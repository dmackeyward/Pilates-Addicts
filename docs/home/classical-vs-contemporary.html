Classical vs. Contemporary Pilates

When exploring Pilates, you will often hear movements described across two main frameworks: Classical and Contemporary. While both share fundamental principles of breath, core control, and alignment, they approach movement from slightly different perspectives.

Classical Pilates: The Foundation

Developed by Joseph Pilates in the 1920s, Classical Pilates preserves the original exercise repertoire and traditional sequence.

  • The Goal: Master a structured, time-tested sequence of movements designed to build holistic strength, control, and spinal mobility.
  • The Style: Systematic, dynamic, and deeply rooted in original form.

Contemporary Pilates: The Evolution

Contemporary Pilates takes those foundational classical exercises and integrates modern biomechanics, exercise science, and physical therapy principles.

  • The Goal: Adapt movements to support individual bodies, address postural imbalances, manage injuries, and build functional strength.
  • The Style: Flexible, adaptive, and focused on motor control and real-world transferability.

How We Teach

We don't view these two approaches as competing methods, but rather as complementary tools. By grounding our practice in the rich tradition of Classical Pilates while leveraging Contemporary biomechanical insights, we ensure every exercise on the reformer is both deeply effective and safely tailored to your unique body.
