What is Pilates?

Pilates is a low-impact movement system designed to strengthen the core, improve posture, and enhance mobility through mindful, controlled exercise. It is built around breath, alignment, balance, and precision.

Key Principles

  • Guided by Breath: Deep breathing supports core engagement and controls movement.
  • Quality over Quantity: Control and precision are prioritized over speed or high repetitions.
  • Functional Mobility: Spinal articulation and joint mobility are central to fluid movement.
  • Personalized Practice: Exercises are adaptable to meet individual needs and goals.

The History of Pilates

Pilates was developed in the early 20th century by German physical trainer Joseph Pilates. Having suffered from asthma and rheumatic fever as a child, Joseph dedicated his youth to studying human anatomy, gymnastics, boxing, and Eastern movement practices to build his physical resilience.

From WWI Rehabilitation to "Contrology"

During World War I, Joseph was interned in England, where he worked as a nurse assisting bedridden soldiers. He rigged springs to hospital beds so patients could exercise using resistance while lying down—an innovation that directly inspired modern Pilates equipment. He originally named his system Contrology, defined as the art of controlled movement to cultivate physical and mental harmony.

The NYC Studio & The Dance Connection

In 1926, Joseph and his wife Clara emigrated to New York City and opened a studio near the New York City Ballet. World-renowned dancers quickly discovered that his method was unmatched for rehabilitation, injury prevention, and building core stability without adding bulky muscle mass.

How Pilates Is Practiced Today

While the core principles remain unchanged, Pilates is primarily practiced in two main formats:

Aspect Mat Pilates Reformer Pilates
Primary Resistance Bodyweight & gravity (plus small props) Springs, straps, and a sliding carriage
Best For Building foundational core control anywhere Targeted resistance, guided alignment & scaling
Accessibility Requires minimal equipment Practiced in specialized studios or home setups

The Modern State of Pilates

Today, Pilates has evolved far beyond its traditional roots into a global movement phenomenon. Contemporary instructors blend Joseph’s original sequence with modern sports science, physical therapy insights, and functional movement principles.

Whether practiced as a standalone discipline, a complement to athletic training, or as part of injury recovery, modern Pilates welcomes movers of all ages, body types, and fitness backgrounds.
