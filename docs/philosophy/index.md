# My Teaching Philosophy

## Movement with Intention: Mind, Muscle, and Breath

Pilates is far more than just going through the physical motions or burning through repetitions — it is a practice of active presence. My goal as an instructor is to help you build a deep, intuitive connection between your mind and body so that every movement on and off the reformer serves a clear purpose.

### Mindfulness & Presence

Stepping onto the reformer is an invitation to tune out external noise. By focusing entirely on form, sensation, and spatial awareness, exercise becomes a moving meditation.

### Intentionally Directed Breathing

Breath is the engine of control. We use deliberate breathing patterns to recruit the deep inner core, regulate the nervous system, manage intra-abdominal pressure, and flow effortlessly through transition states.

### True Muscle Recruitment

Moving quickly often masks muscle compensations. We slow down movement where necessary to ensure you are truly feeling, isolating, and engaging the target muscles rather than letting momentum or global muscle groups take over.

### Length & Dynamic Mobility

True strength requires elasticity. We combine active muscular engagement with intentional lengthening to create open, resilient joints and dynamic flexibility.

### Functional Transferability

What we build on the carriage directly impacts how you move in daily life. Whether you are running, lifting, playing sports, or carrying groceries, our sessions are designed to cultivate posture, efficiency, and longevity for everyday life.

## Understanding Our Approach: Classical vs. Contemporary Pilates

When exploring Pilates, you will often hear movements described across two main frameworks: Classical and Contemporary. While both share the same fundamental principles of breath, core control, and alignment, they approach movement from slightly different perspectives.

### Classical Pilates: The Foundation

Developed by Joseph Pilates in the 1920s, Classical Pilates preserves the original repertoire and order of exercises.

- **The Goal:** Master a structured, time-tested sequence of movements designed to build holistic strength, control, and spinal fluid mobility.
- **The Style:** Systematic, dynamic, and rooted in traditional form.

### Contemporary (Modern) Pilates: The Evolution

Contemporary Pilates takes those foundational classical exercises and integrates modern biomechanics, exercise science, and physical therapy principles.

- **The Goal:** Adapt movements to fit individual bodies, address postural habits, manage injuries, and build functional strength for daily life.
- **The Style:** Flexible, adaptive, and focused on motor control and real-world transferability.

### How We Teach

We don't view these two approaches as competing methods, but rather as complementary tools.

By grounding our practice in the rich tradition of Classical Pilates while leveraging Contemporary biomechanical insight, we ensure every exercise on the reformer is both deeply effective and safely tailored to your unique body.
